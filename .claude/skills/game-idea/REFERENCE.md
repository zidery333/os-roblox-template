# Game ideas that can reach the top of Roblox: the method

Checked 2026-11-15. Roblox's ranking rules changed about five times in 20 months, so re-read the dated numbers before each game.

## The short version

- Borrow a loop that holds players on Roblox today, change one thing the player does, and give it its own name and art. Most hits with a known origin were made this way.
- Test cheap: one action, grey boxes, no models. If the person does not want another go, drop it.
- A new game is shown first only to age-checked players aged 16+. It must be fun for them, and for the person making it.
- The idea stage ends at a small public game that holds players without help. Both record hits needed a team after that point.
- The odds are low and unpublished. Plan many small shots and keep one lesson from each.

## Steps

1. **Read the live charts.** The AI pulls Top Playing Now, Up-and-Coming and Top Trending from Roblox's chart feed, then looks up each riser's like share, created date and server size. Done when: a dated list of 10 risers with those fields exists. Confidence: high.

2. **Shortlist three loops, and skip the current number one.** Pick loops that have risen for a while, or small well-liked games nobody has remixed. Copies of a number one appear within a week, and an AI-built egg follower lost about 94% of its peak in 28 days. Done when: three loops are each tied to a named game. Confidence: medium.

3. **The person plays them.** Ten minutes each on their phone, one line each: the thrill, and would they open it again. For the loop they like, they play the five closest rivals and name what none has. Done when: they can say the missing thing in one sentence. If not, there is no twist yet. Confidence: medium. (`SKILL.md` replaces this step: many people can't name a gap, but judge well by playing and giving notes.)

4. **Write the idea card,** one line each:
   - the game the loop comes from, and the one change in what the player does (not only the skin)
   - the one verb (pull, steal, grow)
   - what the player is doing at second 60, with no tutorial
   - the one picture that shows it truthfully
   - what brings them back tomorrow, the week-3 goal, and whether progress is saved
   - what one player alone does, and what bots do in empty places
   - the weekly "new thing", and how many models one drop needs
   - what a player might pay for

   Done when: every line is filled. A blank week-3 line marks a "short ride" and ranks the idea lower; it is a tie-breaker, not a kill. Confidence: high for second 60 and return days (Roblox docs), medium for the rest.

5. **Run five stop checks.** Would the person play it for a week? Own name, art and menus, with no borrowed characters, brands or memes? Would it rate Minimal, Mild or Moderate? Is progress more than "bigger numbers for money"? Can the AI test most of it alone (logic, not feel)? Done when: five yes answers. One no stops the idea. Confidence: high for the copy and rating rules, medium for the rest.

6. **Build the verb only.** Grey boxes; no models, menus or saving. The AI times seconds to first action in a Studio playtest. The person plays about 15 minutes and answers the plan's four questions (a pass needs a yes to "want another go" and at most one "no" in all). Done when: the first action lands inside 60 seconds and it passes.  Otherwise change the idea, not the polish. Confidence: high.

7. **Friend test.** Roblox allows up to 10 similar-aged friends as early testers. Watch one play for 15 minutes without helping. Done when: testers reach the fun unaided. Confidence: medium.

8. **Public 16+ test.** Release with placeholder art; this tier lists no fee. Seed a few hundred plays from friends, or three short clips. No ads unless the game shows zero Home visits after one to two weeks, and only with the person's yes (ads cost real Robux). Wait seven days. Read three things beside the dashboard's similar-game benchmarks: share leaving inside 60 seconds, session length, next-day return. Done when: seven days of data are read. Confidence: medium.

9. **Decide with two keys.** Shelve only if the numbers are weak and the person does not want to play it. Allow one fix pass and one re-seed, then put it on hold, not delete. If it holds players, fix the core before adding content. Done when: the decision and one lesson are written down. Confidence: medium; no hit maker gives a kill number.

10. **Only now spend.** Make models, bank four to six content drops, then go for the all-ages gate. Models and ads can cost real money: only with the person's own accounts, and ask before any paid step. Done when: 250 highly engaged plays show in the Audience Reach panel. Confidence: high for the rule, low for how one person gets there.

## What needs the person themselves

Times marked ~ are a checker's estimate, not sourced.

- Once: age check and two-factor login, ~10-15 min. Set weekly hours, 1 min. Write the "500 steady players" rule, ~10 min.
- Weekly: play three risers, 30 min.
- Per idea: taste yes or no, 1 min. Play five rivals, ~1 hour.
- Per prototype: fun check, about 15 min. Friend test, ~1-1.5 hours.
- Per public build: phone test, 10 min. Content questionnaire, 5-10 min. Ad set-up and payment, ~15 min (card payment needs age 18+; ads cost real money and need the person's own account). Three clips, ~30 min.
- Per data check: export or show the dashboard, 5 min.
- After a pass: pick each model in a model chat the person starts.

## Never do

- **Same-name, same-look copies.** Roblox stops prioritising them. Of 105 "Steal a" games, the original held about 92% of players. Its owners have filed at least four lawsuits over near-copies.
- **Borrowed characters, brands or memes.** One person cannot absorb a takedown, and memes die: the biggest brainrot follower fell from a reported 5M players to about 4,400.
- **Spend on models, fees or ads before a pass.** Ad players never count in ranking and are generally not shown the game on Home again.
- **Kill a game for zero players in week one.** One solo maker reported nine days of zero Home impressions, then traction near week three. Roblox says a slow start carries no penalty.
- **Hard-code a retention pass mark.** The "20% good, 40% excellent" charts come from small samples of promoted games. Nobody publishes what 50 to 200 test players should show.
- **Judge ideas by likes, visits or players at once.** None is a Home signal.
- **Fake game pages with ads.** No published results, and the page must match the real game.

## Where good sources disagree

- **New or remix.** Roblox funds "novel" games and calls reskins a hard sell. The charts show remixes winning. Grow a Garden did bring a mechanic new to Roblox: growth while offline.
- **Data or taste.** Agencies gate ideas on metrics. Onett and the 99 Nights lead say data misleads; 99 Nights had weak early numbers.
- **When to kill.** Six months with no players (Jandel), five to seven days (a forum guide), one month (Twin Atlas).
- **Ads.** Roblox staff cite games that spent under $100. Over 70% of the top 100 earners run ads.
- **Hook time.** Roblox's retention page says five minutes. Its ranker counts 60 and 180 seconds. Use 60.
- **Older players.** Roblox says RPG, strategy and shooter games are in short supply and pays 42% more on age-checked US adult spend in eligible games. Naavik calls the showcase shooter's numbers very modest. Unproven.
- **Phones.** 80% mobile (Roblox, 2024) against 11.6% phone-only (GameAnalytics, 2026). Build for thumbs either way.

## What nobody knows yet

- The odds of a new game reaching 1,000 players.
- The hit rate of "remix with a twist". All the evidence comes from winners.
- How a solo maker with no following gets the first 250 engaged 16+ plays, and which traffic counts.
- Whether one person with AI can keep weekly updates going.
- Which loop type decays slowest. Every 2025-2026 spike fell 90% or more within six months.
- Whether a fee is needed to reach ages 9-15. Roblox's two publishing pages disagree.
- Whether clips from a new account bring players.
- Not researched: paid random item rules, age-banded chat, translation.
